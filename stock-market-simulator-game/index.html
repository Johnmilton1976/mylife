Stock Market Simulator Game

Most stock market games stop at a chart: buy low, sell high, watch a number climb. This one asks a better question — what does the portfolio actually do for your life? In MY LIFE, a fictional market runs inside a whole city. You buy dividend stocks with cash you earned, collect a payout every time your walk brings you past them, and reinvest until your passive income covers your bills. It is a free browser stock market simulator with no download, no account and no real money at stake.

One complete trade, in real numbers

Trades execute at the displayed price with no commission and no spread — what you see is what you pay. Say you arrive at an office tower with $2,000 and buy MRDN (Meridian Bank) at its $245.30 opening price:

  • How many shares? $2,000 ÷ $245.30 = 8 whole shares (the game buys integer quantities only), costing $1,962.40 and leaving $37.60 cash.
  • What does it pay? MRDN yields 2.3%, so 8 shares × $245.30 × 2.3% = about $45.14 of dividend per year, credited to cash in monthly slices of roughly $3.76 as the game's month turns.
  • What if the price moves? If MRDN rises to $260, your 8 shares are worth $2,080 on the ledger — an $117.60 paper gain. If it falls to $220, the same shares read $1,760. The dividend keeps paying in both cases, which is exactly the point the game is making.
  • Why average cost matters: buy 8 shares at $245.30, then 8 more at $220, and your recorded cost per share becomes $232.65 — the game tracks a running weighted average, and sells are valued at the current price, not what you paid.

Now run the same $2,000 into MDIX (Medix Labs) at $28.40 with its 5.5% yield: 70 shares cost $1,988, and the annual dividend is about $109.34 — roughly $9.11 a month. Same money, very different cash flow. That trade-off is the core decision the market loop exists to teach.

When dividends cover your bills

Rent in the game is $950/month, with food, transit and phone adding roughly another $665 — about $1,615 of baseline monthly outgoings. The win condition is not a big number in a chart; it is dividend income plus rent and business income rising until that line covers the bills. A portfolio heavy in the high-yield payers (MDIX at 5.5%, DRCK at 3.4%, SODA at 3.0%) moves that line faster than one parked in zero-yield price bets — but the seeded daily candles mean prices wobble, so timing your buys still matters. You can watch the whole balance sheet, assets against liabilities, any time from the ledger.

Learn investing without risking money

Because every ticker and price is invented, the game teaches the mechanics — dividends, recurring income, reinvestment, the cost of debt — without pretending to be a trading platform. You can make the same mistake a real investor makes (buying a liability that quietly drains cash) and feel the consequence in a few minutes instead of a few years. It is a sandbox for the habit, not a substitute for financial advice.

Investing inside a whole life, not a spreadsheet

A pure trading sim isolates the market. MY LIFE puts it back in context: you still need a job, rent, food and a sane stress level. A portfolio that pays well is only useful if you can hold it together long enough to collect. That is why the game pairs its market with mood, health and the 19 life goals — the investing is part of a life, not the whole of one.

Frequently asked

Is this a real-money stock trading game? No. Every stock and price is fictional and has no real-world value. It simulates how investing works — dividends, cash flow, compound growth — without real money or a brokerage account.

What does a stock market simulator teach here? That an asset pays you repeatedly, not just once. Dividend stocks credit cash every time your route brings you past them, so you can watch passive income grow and eventually cover your monthly bills.

Is it free and browser-based? Yes. It runs in any browser with no download, no account, and no in-game ads. Your progress stays in your own browser.
