Real Life Simulator

"Real life simulator" gets used two ways, and the difference decides what you get. One camp means real-world statistics — a game built on census and World Bank data, where you are randomly born somewhere and live the odds. The other means real mechanics — money that behaves the way money actually does. MY LIFE is firmly in the second camp, and this page explains why that version is the one that teaches anything.

Real statistics vs real mechanics

A statistics game shows you the world's inequality; a mechanics game lets you practice inside it. A stats game is a documentary you click through. A mechanics game is a workshop. MY LIFE picks the workshop: invented places, but real rules for cash, debt, and assets — the parts of life that actually move a balance sheet.

What "real" money looks like in the game

Why mechanics beat statistics for learning

You can read that most people never escape the rat race, or you can play the moment your dividend finally covers the rent. The second one changes behaviour; the first one just informs it. A simulator earns the word "real" when the lesson lands in your hand, not just in your head.

Where MY LIFE sits

It is a browser hybrid — spin, walk a Manhattan grid, land on buildings that are choices, and a ledger tracks it all. Real money rules, an invented city, and the Rich Dad loop, free with no account. The city map is the menu; the rules are the engine.
