The Rich Dad Rules MY LIFE Plays By

MY LIFE is a free life simulator game built around one book: Rich Dad Poor Dad. The whole game is the book's argument turned into mechanics — you don't read the rule, you live inside it and feel it on your cash-flow sheet every month. Here is every rule the game encodes, with the exact numbers it uses.

Rule 1 — The only rule: assets put money in your pocket; liabilities take money out.
The price tag decides nothing; the direction of cash flow decides everything. A $780,000 penthouse bought as a rental is an asset. A $25,000 car to look successful is a liability — the game's panel files it under liabilities from the day you sign, no matter how good it feels.
Rule 2 — Pay yourself first.
Turn the dial on and 10% of every salary is swept into your asset fund before the bills see a cent. Keep it running three months straight and that alone is a life goal. Miss months and the count resets — paying yourself first is a habit, not an event.
Rule 3 — Good debt vs bad debt.
The bank lends exactly two kinds of money. Bad debt at 15% APR buys consumption — it becomes a monthly interest bill you feel. Good debt at 6% APR is only approved for buying an asset that yields more than its own interest — the asset pays the interest, you keep the spread. Using good debt once is one of the 14 goals; it's the moment the book clicks.
Rule 4 — Mind your own business.
Your job pays the bills (the E quadrant); your business is the column of assets that grows without your hours. Own a business that runs without you (the B quadrant) and invest in what pays you while you sleep (the I quadrant). The 14 goals literally track which quadrant you're standing in.

What counts as a liability

  • A car — depreciation plus running costs, month after month
  • Bad debt — 15% APR on whatever it bought; the interest is a standing expense
  • Lifestyle inflation — every "small upgrade" you finance raises your baseline bills
  • Burnout — a collapse at work is an ER bill; health is on the books too

The finish line: financial freedom

Freedom is measured, not felt: the game computes the share of your bills covered by passive income, and the ladder runs 25% → 100%. At 25% you bank a goal; at 100% — passive income covering every bill — you are free, and that is the 14th and final goal. When all 14 are banked the city prints your closing card: A LIFE, LIVED.

Every goal, condition and strategy is listed in the 18-goal guide; the numbers above live inside the game, on the streets of the New York map.
