Escape the Rat Race Game

"Rat race" is the picture of working for money, watching expenses rise to meet it, and never quite getting free. Robert Kiyosaki made the phrase central to how a generation thinks about money. An escape-the-rat-race game takes that one sentence and makes it playable: you win not by earning more, but by letting assets pay your bills. Here is how that works as a game — and where MY LIFE runs the loop.

The one rule that ends the rat race

Passive income greater than your expenses. A salary stops being the finish line and becomes the fuel. Until your assets throw off more cash than you spend, you are in the race; the moment they do, you are out. Simple to say, hard to feel — which is exactly why a game helps. The Rich Dad rules page lays out the asset-versus-liability distinction behind it.

How a game teaches it without a lecture

In real life the lesson is invisible. In a game, every purchase is a line item. Buy a liability and a monthly cost appears; buy an asset and a monthly credit appears. You watch a raise get eaten by a bigger apartment, and watch a small dividend stack into freedom. The arithmetic does the teaching, and you remember it because you lived the deduction.

What MY LIFE's loop looks like

You take a job for a salary, walk Manhattan to spend or invest, and acquire assets that pay monthly cash flow. That flow climbs until it covers your expenses — and the struggle ends. Bank the fourteenth of the 18 goals and the game prints a closing card instead of a score. It is the rat-race rule, run as a browser game: no install, no account, your save stays local.

Why browser and no account helps you finish

The whole point fails if the friction stops you starting. A browser tab with no login removes that wall: open it, play a few minutes, close it, come back tomorrow. Low commitment is what lets the loop actually complete instead of sitting half-played on a phone you forgot to open.
