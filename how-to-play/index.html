How to Play MY LIFE

MY LIFE is a free life simulator you play in a browser — no install, no account, no download. You start as an 18-year-old in New York with $1,000 in your pocket, and the city takes it from there. This guide walks you through everything the game never stops to explain.

1 · Start a life

Press START LIFE, type your name and pick male or female — the game hands you a face at birth. You begin at 18 years old, $1,000 cash, no job, one skill at level 1. The clock starts the moment the city appears: one in-game day passes in a few real seconds, and every 30 days the month settles — salary, bills, dividends and rent all reconcile on your cash-flow sheet.

2 · Spin the wheel, walk the city

The gold wheel (bottom left) is your legs. Tap it or press SPACE: a 1–6 spin decides how many blocks you walk along the route. At some junctions you will randomly turn off for a detour, then rejoin — every street gets visited eventually. Stop on a building cell and that building opens: this is how the whole game works.

  • Companies — find a job or climb the ladder
  • Bank — savings at 3.2% APR, good debt at 6%, bad debt at 15%
  • School / library — study one of 8 skills (tuition rises with level; LV5 is the ceiling)
  • Realty office — buy rentals that pay you rent every month
  • Dealership — buy a car (it is a liability, not an asset)
  • Restaurants, gym, cinema — feed yourself, manage happiness and stress

4 · Work, study, and keep the body honest

There are 29 jobs across 8 skills (programming, writing, project management, driving, cooking, art, finance, fitness), each with its own ladder — from IT SUPPORT at $4,200/month up to EDITOR-IN-CHIEF at $10,400 and HEAD OF ENGINEERING at $16,500. At LV5 any skill becomes a WORK: make it once — a novel, an app, a print series, a sauce line — and it pays a fixed royalty every month. And past LV5 the Kiyosaki leap can happen: once the work is out in the world, a rare BIG BREAK lucky card can land — a Fortune 500 licence for your app, a bestseller list for your novel, an auction hammer for your art — bringing a windfall, a newspaper headline, and a large permanent monthly income. Working consecutive days builds tenure and performance; push too hard and stress climbs, health pays the bill, and a collapse sends you to the emergency room. Study raises skill levels, but over-studying raises stress. Reputation is the one pay rise nobody can take back.

5 · Build cash flow, not a garage

The game plays by one rule (borrowed from the Rich Dad): assets put money in your pocket every month; liabilities take money out. Dividend stocks, positive-cash-flow rentals and a business that runs without you are assets. The car you bought to look successful is a liability — and so is any debt that bought consumption (15% APR). Debt that buys an asset yielding more than its 6% APR is called good debt, and using it once is a badge. The full breakdown lives in our Rich Dad rules guide.

6 · Chase the 18 goals

Eighteen milestones take you from landing your first job to a billion dollars of net worth. The first 14 are the life goals, and the fourteenth — passive income covering every bill — is financial freedom: bank all 14 and the city prints your closing card, A LIFE, LIVED. The four rungs above it are the fortune ladder, measured in net worth alone. Every goal, with the exact condition and a strategy for reaching it, is in the 18-goal list.

Tips for your first month

  1. Land any job in your first week — salary is the seed money of everything else.
  2. Turn on pay yourself first: 10% of every salary is swept into your asset fund before bills see it.
  3. Buy your first dividend stock before you buy anything with a logo on it.
  4. Zoom out once in a while — the New York map is worth seeing whole.
  5. Press 💾 before you close the tab. The city keeps no copies.
